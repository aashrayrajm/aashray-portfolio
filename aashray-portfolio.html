Frequently Asked Questions
What is your design process? +

I start with the human, not the brief. I get as close to the real user as possible — field interviews, on-site observation, direct conversation. Then I find the simplest solution that genuinely works. Not the most impressive one. From there: wireframes, iteration, high-fidelity — staying connected to the user throughout.

What roles are you looking for? +

Senior Product Designer, Lead Product Designer, or Head of Design — at startups or scale-ups building AI-powered products. I work best close to users, across the full product lifecycle, collaborating directly with founders or product leads.

How do you approach designing AI products? +

AI products fail when users don't trust them. I make AI feel predictable and honest — not magical. I've designed for construction teams who are deeply skeptical of technology. That taught me to surface AI outputs clearly, give users control, and never hide uncertainty behind a clean UI.

What makes your research different? +

I've researched users who have never spoken to a computer — construction workers on US job sites, respiratory patients in rural India, physiotherapy patients in Secunderabad. Their behavior is in no dataset. Understanding them required physical presence, trust, and time. That's different from running a usability test.

What tools do you use? +

Figma for design. Shadcn UI for design systems. UX Pilot and Claude for AI-assisted workflows. Framer for portfolio. Adobe Creative Suite and Canva for marketing. Jira, Confluence, and Notion for collaboration.

AI SaaS · Lead Designer · Aug 2024 – Feb 2025

Designing AI that Construction Teams Actually Trust

End-to-end design of an AI document intelligence platform — reducing contract review time by 70% and doubling delivery speed. Solo designer, from research to launch.

Construction Tech / AI SaaS
Lead Designer (Solo)
Aug 2024 – Feb 2025
1 Business Analyst · 1 Designer

Challenge

Construction teams manage hundreds of contracts — often 300–500 pages each. Missing one clause can cost hundreds of thousands of dollars. Most teams still reviewed documents manually with no workflow, no AI assistance, and no audit trail.

Results

Teams handled 2–3× more bids without adding headcount. First-time AI users navigated confidently from session one — because the interface made AI feel trustworthy, not intimidating.

70% ↓
Time saved on contract generation
60% ↓
Reduction in drafting effort
2× ↑
Contract delivery speed

Process

Research & Analysis

2 years of embedded experience with construction back-office teams before this project. Focused interviews on document workflows. Key finding: 80% of the friction was in reading, not writing.

AI Sidebar — Always Visible

Summarize, Translate, Compare, and Checkpoints accessible at any point. No hidden features. The sidebar stays open because the user should never have to think about how to access AI.

Risk Score on Every Document

AI generates a compliance risk score based on custom checklists. Users see it immediately on open. In construction, you need to know the risk before you start reading — not after.

Prompt Templates

Users saved frequent AI queries as reusable templates. One of the biggest workflow wins discovered through user research — reducing repeated typing for common document tasks.

Approval Workflow in the Document

Drafting → Internal Review → Compliance Validation → Approval. All within the same screen. No email chains. No context switching.

Empty States as Trust Signals

Every empty state communicates what AI will do next — not just that nothing is there yet. This single decision reduced confusion for first-time AI users significantly.

Building a Construction OS from 0 to $2.4M

Designed the entire Inncircles platform over 5 years — 1000+ screens, a full design system, and a mobile-first field app. The only designer selected to fly to the US and onboard $500M+ enterprise clients.

Construction SaaS
Designer → Product Associate → Senior Designer
Aug 2021 – Present
6 Designers (Design System)

Challenge

Construction projects fail because of people problems. Field teams, consultants, and back-office staff working in silos — manual processes, no real-time visibility, constant rework. 60% of delays from poor field execution visibility. 40% from miscommunication.

Results

90% real-time progress tracking. 100% site documentation centralized. Zero manual follow-ups. 2–3 hours of daily time saved per team through automated inspections and task assignment.

Process

The American Construction Worker Insight

On US job sites, field workers needed interfaces that were slower, clearer, with fewer decisions per screen. Gloves on. Sun in their eyes. High distraction. One primary action per screen. Tap-and-submit over form entry. Daily work log submissions went from inconsistent to near-100% compliance.

Simple Forms as Features

The smallest UX change had the largest field impact. Replaced form input with button-based selection. A simple form became the most important feature for field workers — because it matched how they actually worked.

Design System — Built from Scratch

Token architecture: Primitive → Global → Semantic. 25+ components rebuilt with full variant coverage. Referenced Atlassian, Shopify, Razorpay, Salesforce. Result: 30% reduction in screen design time. 35% faster dev handoffs.

End-to-End Ownership

Bridge between construction clients and engineering in India. Managed requirements, translated client needs into dev-ready specs, handled deployments, adjusted to US timezones. Only person from the company selected to fly to the US for enterprise onboarding.

"The work log button-to-tap transformation wasn't a design decision. It was a human insight that became one. That's the difference."

I Was 20. COVID Was Closing Hospitals. So I Built a Diagnostic Device.

A wearable AI device that detects lung diseases through breath sounds — built-in stethoscope, Google ML model, mobile app. Built in 2020. Patent published in India.

Healthcare · IoT · AI · Mobile
Sole Designer & Inventor
2020
Indian Patent Office · Road to Shine Japan · T-Hub

Challenge

COVID made hospitals inaccessible. People with respiratory symptoms had no way to self-diagnose. 78% of people surveyed struggled to access respiratory care. 52% couldn't recognize early symptoms. No affordable home solution existed.

Results

A working AI-powered wearable detecting asthma, bronchitis, and respiratory distress at home. Selected to represent India at T-Hub and Hiroshima Prefecture Japan. Published patent. Government recognition from Telangana State.

Published
Indian Patent Office Journal 32/2020
1st Place

How It Works

The vest contains a built-in stethoscope that captures lung sounds. Audio feeds through a microphone into a mobile app running Google's open-source ML model — trained to detect wheezes, crackles, and respiratory patterns associated with asthma, bronchitis, and other conditions. The app surfaces a clear result with plain-language next steps. No doctor. No hospital.

Research & Design

200+ Surveys

Urban and rural patients about respiratory access challenges. 78% struggled to access care. 65% overly relied on specialists. 52% didn't recognize early symptoms — meaning the device needed to catch what users couldn't.

20+ Interviews

In-person interviews with patients and healthcare professionals. Key insight: people need results they can understand, not medical jargon. The app had to be plain, clear, and actionable.

App Design Principles

One action per screen. No medical jargon. Clear visual feedback: plug in → wear → breathe → wait → result. Colour-coded results (Normal, Crackles, Wheezes) with plain-language explanations and clear next steps.

Road to Shine — Japan

Selected for the India-Japan human-centered innovation program. Collaborated with DSI Technologies and Hiroshima Prefecture. Won 1st place in Hyderabad, represented India at T-Hub and Hiroshima.

"No company, no brief, no budget. Just a problem and the determination to solve it with the simplest thing that worked. At 20."
